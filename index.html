Executive Briefing · International Sailing Governance

How World Sailing, MNAs, and Olympic Committees Intersect Across Athlete Eligibility and Competition Pathways

A neutral, globally applicable model of how authority is distributed between the IOC, World Sailing, continental confederations, Member National Authorities (MNAs / NGBs), and National Olympic Committees (NOCs) — and how those lines shift between routine training, sanctioned competition, and Games representation. Built from primary sources. Confirmed facts are separated from common practice and from open questions.

Confidence: Confirmed — cite-able in a primary document Common international practice — typical but not necessarily codified Varies by federation or local bylaw Open question — requires clarification from World Sailing

01Governance Hierarchy

Two parallel chains of authority — the Sport chain (IOC → IF → MNA → clubs/athletes) and the Olympic chain (IOC → NOC → Games representation). They are distinct. They intersect only at specific points, principally at Games selection and at nationality decisions.

Sport Chain — authority over the sport

IOC Recognises the IF · sets Olympic-eligibility framework
World Sailing (IF) One recognised MNA per country · owns the Eligibility Code · sanctions World/Continental titles
MNA / NGB (e.g. US Sailing, VISA, RYA) Membership · domestic sanctioning · enters competitors into WS events
Clubs · Class associations · Athletes Daily training, club racing, qualifying regattas

Olympic Chain — authority over Games representation

IOC Owns the Olympic Charter · recognises the NOC · sets Rule 41 (nationality)
Continental Body (Panam Sports · CAC · etc.) Owns Pan Am / CAC / Junior Pan Am Games · own nationality rules mirror Rule 41
NOC (e.g. USOPC, VIOC, BOA) Exclusive authority to enter the country at Games · approves athlete delegation
Athlete · Games Delegation Olympic Games · Pan American Games · CAC Games · Youth Olympic Games
Where the chains intersect. (1) At Games entry: the NOC enters the athlete into the Olympic/Pan Am/CAC/YOG, but only on the proposal of the MNA and only if WS / the IF certifies eligibility. (2) At nationality changes: a transfer between countries requires agreement from both NOCs and the IF, with the IOC/Panam Sports Executive Board as final approver. (3) At shared anti-doping and SafeSport obligations: WADA Code and IOC ethics standards bind both chains.

02The Five Governance Actors

Each box below describes one actor: its purpose, the source of its authority, who it governs, what events it controls, and whether it touches participation, eligibility, selection, nationality, residency, or competition.

Apex of the Olympic Movement

International Olympic Committee (IOC) Established 1894 · Lausanne, Switzerland

Purpose
Supreme authority of the Olympic Movement; owner of the Olympic Charter; owner of the Olympic Games.
Authority over
Confirmed Recognising IFs (World Sailing) and NOCs (e.g. USOPC, VIOC); approving nationality changes for the Olympic Games under Rule 41Olympic Charter Rule 41.
Governs (directly)
NOCs · IFs · the Olympic Games and Youth Olympic Games.
Events controlled
Summer and Winter Olympic Games · Youth Olympic Games · IOC-patronised Regional Games.
Touches athlete eligibility?
Confirmed Only for Olympic / Youth Olympic Games. Rule 41: athlete must be a national of the NOC entering them; 3-year wait after representing another country at Olympics / regional games / IF world or regional championships — IOC Executive Board may reduce or waive with NOC + IF agreementBye-law to Rule 41 §2.
Touches MNA participation?
No direct authority outside Games-eligibility nationality rules. Day-to-day MNA participation is not governed by the IOC.
International Federation for Sailing

World Sailing Recognised by the IOC as the sole IF for sailing

Purpose
Worldwide governance of the sport of sailing: rules of racing, classes, eligibility, world-title events, Olympic qualification.
Authority over
Confirmed Constitution Aim (a): "the controlling authority of the sport of sailing in all its forms throughout the world." Aim (f): "control, organise, conduct, license or sanction other championships, sailing events or activities." Article 1.1: a National Authority is an organisation controlling sailing "in any country or in any political entity granted status as an Olympic nation." Article 4: Full Membership is granted to such a National Authority; Associate Membership covers territories not IOC-recognised — meaning two distinct membership tracks, with full-MNA standing reserved for IOC-recognised political entities. WS owns the Eligibility Code (Regulation 19); appoints international juries / umpires / officials for events under its sanction.
Governs (directly)
MNAs · WS classes · WS-titled events · Olympic Sailing Competition (in coordination with the IOC).
Events controlled
The Olympic Sailing Competition · World Championships of Olympic classes · the Youth Sailing World Championships · Continental Championships (for sanction purposes) · "World Sailing" titled events.
Touches athlete eligibility?
Confirmed Reg. 19.4: "WS Eligibility" requires MNA membership + WS Sailor registration + acceptance of WS regulations. Reg. 19.10: The country a competitor represents is the country of the MNA that entered them. Reg. 19.14: A competitor under 16 is not regarded as having represented a country. Reg. 19.15: 3-year wait for transfer; WS Board may reduce/cancel with agreement of MNAs concerned.
Touches NOC participation?
Common practice At Games, the NOC enters athletes but only those proposed by the MNA and certified WS-eligible. WS does not control the NOC's internal delegation decisions.
Continental Olympic confederation

Continental Body e.g. Panam Sports · CACSO · OCA · EOC · ANOCA

Purpose
Operates continental and sub-regional multi-sport Games under IOC patronage. Owns the Pan American Games, Junior Pan American Games, CAC Games.
Authority over
Confirmed Panam Sports Constitution §34.4–34.7: Competitor must be a national of the NOC entering them; 3-year wait after representing another country at Pan Am / Olympic Games / IF world or regional championships — Panam Sports Executive Board may reduce/waive with NOC + Confederation agreement.
Governs (directly)
NOCs of its continent · the continental Sport Confederations (e.g. PASAF for sailing).
Events controlled
Pan American Games (17–22 in Junior edition, year of birth-defined) · Junior Pan American Games · CAC Games · CAC Junior Games · their qualification regattas.
Touches eligibility?
Yes — for its own Games only. Mirrors Rule 41 in structure.
Touches MNA participation?
No direct authority over MNA-internal participation.
Member National Authority / NGB

MNA (e.g. VISA, US Sailing) The recognised national federation for sailing in its territory

Purpose
National governance of the sport: membership, domestic racing, athlete development, ranking, selection for international events.
Authority over
Confirmed Membership of clubs and individual sailors; sanctioning domestic events; entering competitors into World Sailing events (the entry act establishes the country a sailor represents — WS Reg. 19.10). WS Constitution Schedule A, Group O expressly lists "Virgin Islands Sailing Association (US)" as a Full MNA — independent of US Sailing, which is in Group P (North America). The two are recognised by WS as separate national authorities for separate political entities. Varies over residency, eligibility, transfer — most MNA bylaws delegate to WS Reg. 19, but the recognised MNA may impose additional internal selection criteria.
Governs (directly)
Member clubs · class associations · individual members · domestic regattas.
Events controlled
National Championships · MNA-sanctioned regattas · MNA-designated trial / Olympic-qualifying events within its jurisdiction.
Touches eligibility / selection?
Confirmed (for the sport chain) VISA Bylaws §3.3, 3.5: maintain Full Member status with World Sailing and respect its rules. VISA Bylaws §3.8: may arrange and conduct races. VISA bylaws do not impose citizenship or residency requirements on individual sailors. Open — selection authority for Games-pathway events is typically a joint MNA/NOC matter and varies.
Touches NOC representation?
The MNA proposes athletes to the NOC for Games. The NOC decides the entry.
National Olympic Committee

NOC (e.g. VIOC, USOPC) The IOC-recognised representative of a territory in the Olympic Movement

Purpose
Develop and protect the Olympic Movement within the territory; lead the national delegation to Games.
Authority over
Confirmed (VIOC bylaws §2.3(b)) "Exclusive authority for the representation of the US Virgin Islands at the Olympic Games and at the regional, continental or world multi-sports competitions patronised by the IOC." The list named: Summer and Winter Olympics, Pan American Games, CAC Games, Youth Olympic Games, other Regional Games.
Governs (directly)
Member national federations (one per sport, §3.1(d)); Games delegations.
Events controlled
Entry into Olympic Games · Pan American Games · CAC Games · Youth Olympic Games · IOC-patronised Regional Games. The NOC does not own World Championships, continental class championships, MNA events, or non-IOC events.
Touches eligibility?
Confirmed (VIOC §9.1(b)) Residency for Games representation: born in VI, OR parent born in VI, OR U.S. citizen who has resided 3 years continuously in VI before the Opening Ceremony. Confirmed (VIOC §9.1(a) final sentence) "The Executive Committee shall make provisions for those athletes under 18 years of age."
Touches MNA participation?
Open — the bylaws give the NOC exclusive authority over Games representation, not over MNA-level participation in non-Games competition. Whether the NOC's residency rules also condition MNA membership or non-Games WS-event entry is not addressed in the VIOC bylaws and is a key question for World Sailing.

05Under-18 Athlete Considerations

Minors typically receive specific protections and carve-outs across the system. The key under-18 levers are international (WS, IOC); local under-18 provisions vary by NOC bylaw.

What is confirmed

  • Confirmed WS Regulation 19.14 — "A competitor shall not be regarded as having represented a country unless he is at least 16 years of age at the time." This is the most important under-18 lever in international sailing: it means competition before age 16 does not create a "represented country" history that would otherwise trigger Reg. 19.15's 3-year transfer wait or IOC Rule 41's 3-year wait.
  • Confirmed VIOC Bylaws §9.1(a) (final sentence) — "The Executive Committee shall make provisions for those athletes under 18 years of age." The bylaws explicitly contemplate Executive Committee discretion for minors in respect of residency.
  • Confirmed VISA Bylaws §4.1(e) recognise "junior" individual members but do not impose distinct eligibility provisions inside the MNA itself.
  • Confirmed Youth Sailing World Championships NoR §3.6 (2026) — ages 12 to under 19. The MNA enters competitors. No NOC approval is required for the Youth Worlds.

Common international practice

  • Common practice MNAs and NOCs typically apply a more flexible residency test for minors — recognising that a 12- or 14-year-old's "domicile" follows the parent / guardian, not a 3-year personal clock the minor cannot independently satisfy.
  • Common practice Youth-only events (Optimist Worlds, Class Junior Worlds, Youth Sailing Worlds) are MNA-entered, not NOC-entered. Many NOCs play no role in youth international competition.
  • Common practice Where a youth athlete is a national of the country of the new MNA, transfer in the sport chain at the MNA level is administratively simple. The Games-level eligibility clock is a separate question that runs in parallel.

Areas that vary

  • Varies Whether a specific NOC has published a written youth-specific residency provision (most NOCs delegate to executive discretion as VIOC does).
  • Varies Whether an MNA imposes additional internal "selection" criteria above WS Eligibility for youth athletes representing the MNA at WS events.
  • Varies Whether continental confederations (Panam Sports, CACSO) treat youth athletes who later age into senior pathways under a separate transition rule.

Where World Sailing policy does override local interpretation

  • For events that fall within Regulation 19.6 (WS events, Olympic Sailing, IOC Regional Games, WS-titled events, world/continental championships of Olympic classes), WS Eligibility is the binding standard. The MNA's recognition by WS is the act that establishes the country a sailor represents at those events (Reg. 19.10).
  • For nationality and transfer questions involving a WS event, WS Reg. 19.18 places the determination with the WS Board; an alleged breach is not grounds for protest under RRS 63.1.
  • For under-16 athletes, Reg. 19.14 applies regardless of local interpretation — a minor under 16 has not "represented" a country, period.
  • WS recognition of an MNA is itself decisive: WS Constitution Article 1.1 defines a National Authority as the organisation controlling sailing "in any country or in any political entity granted status as an Olympic nation." Under this definition, an IOC-recognised territory has its own Full MNA — distinct from the MNA of the sovereign country to which it is politically attached. Schedule A codifies this: VISA appears in Group O (NA, CA and Caribbean) and US Sailing in Group P (North America). The two MNAs are peers in the WS membership structure, not parent and subsidiary.

06Key Questions for World Sailing

These questions cannot be conclusively answered from the public bylaws and regulations alone. They are the points that warrant clarification from World Sailing's Governance team before any specific pathway decision is finalised.

1

Does an MNA independently control athlete participation outside Games environments?

Open Public WS regulations describe the MNA as the recognised national authority and the entrant at WS events. They do not address whether an MNA's participation pathways (membership, domestic racing, selection for non-Games WS events) can be conditioned by an NOC's Games-level eligibility timeline. WS clarification: where does MNA autonomy end?

2

Can an NOC's interpretation effectively control MNA participation pathways?

Open VIOC §2.3(b) gives the NOC "exclusive authority for representation at the Olympic / Pan Am / CAC / Youth Olympic Games." Read narrowly, this is Games-only. Read broadly, it might be cited to condition MNA-internal participation. WS clarification: does WS recognise any extension of NOC authority beyond Games entry?

3

Where does World Sailing authority begin and end relative to an MNA?

Open WS Reg. 19.6 lists the events for which WS Eligibility is required. Outside those, the MNA's domestic authority is largely intact. WS clarification: in what scenarios can WS direct, override, or substitute an MNA's internal determination on athlete participation?

4

For athletes under 18 with no senior representation history, how are transfer and residency situations handled?

Open Reg. 19.14 confirms that under-16 competition does not constitute representation. For 16–17-year-olds with no representation history, the WS Reg. 19.15 transfer mechanics are conceptually moot (nothing to transfer from). WS clarification: confirm that an under-18 with no Reg. 19.10 representation record is unconstrained by Reg. 19.15 waiting periods at the WS-events layer.

5

Can an athlete train, compete locally, compete internationally, and participate in WS events while completing residency timelines for Games eligibility?

Open The architecture suggests yes — the sport chain (WS / MNA) and the Olympic chain (IOC / NOC) run in parallel, and an athlete's residency clock under an NOC's Games-eligibility provision does not, on its face, bar WS-event entry by the recognised MNA. WS clarification: please confirm in writing that the residency clock for an NOC's Games representation runs in parallel with — not as a precondition for — WS-event participation by the same athlete.

6

What is the proper venue if an MNA and an NOC in the same territory hold conflicting interpretations of athlete eligibility?

Open WS Reg. 19.18 places eligibility determinations at the WS Board. VIOC §10 routes disputes to the General Assembly and then to CAS. The IOC Rule 41 process routes to the IOC Executive Board for Games-level nationality. WS clarification: where there is a conflict between MNA and NOC on a sport-eligibility question, which forum is dispositive — and how does WS step in?

07Scenario Application — neutral first-principles read

The structure above is built to apply to any federation / country / age group. The section below applies that same structure to a real-world fact pattern that motivated this work, without departing from the source documents.

The fact pattern

A youth sailor (under 18) holds U.S. citizenship and is exploring participation under a different MNA / NOC pairing within a U.S. territorial framework (the US Virgin Islands), where VISA is the WS-recognised MNA and VIOC is the IOC-recognised NOC. The dispute concerns whether the athlete can train and compete inside the MNA structure while residency timelines that may apply to Games representation are still running.

A. Sport chain (MNA / WS) — what the documents say

VISA bylaws (§3.1, §4.1) admit clubs, organizations, and individual members without a citizenship or residency test inside the MNA. VISA's authority is to govern, promote, and arrange sailing in its jurisdiction (§3.1, §3.8) and to maintain Full Member status with World Sailing (§3.3, §3.5). World Sailing's own Constitution (Schedule A, Group O) recognises VISA as a Full MNA in its own right, separate from US Sailing. On a plain reading, an athlete may become a VISA member and race in VISA-sanctioned events without first satisfying any Games-level residency clock.

B. Olympic chain (NOC / IOC) — what the documents say

VIOC §2.3(b) gives VIOC exclusive authority for representation at the Olympic / Pan Am / CAC / Youth Olympic Games. That list is bounded. VIOC §9.1(b) imposes a 3-year continuous residency requirement specifically "to represent the Virgin Islands at" those Games. §9.1(a) explicitly directs that "The Executive Committee shall make provisions for those athletes under 18 years of age."

C. World Sailing — what is binding

WS Reg. 19.10 fixes the country a competitor represents at WS events as the country of the MNA that enters them. WS Reg. 19.14 confirms that "a competitor shall not be regarded as having represented a country unless he is at least 16 years of age." WS Reg. 19.15 imposes a 3-year transfer wait — which applies only where prior "representation" exists. For an under-16 with no prior representation, the transfer-waiting machinery has no anchor.

D. Where the two chains actually meet

The chains meet only at the Games entry stage (Stage 7 of the pathway). VIOC's Games-residency clock does not, on the face of the VIOC bylaws, condition MNA-level participation. Two interpretations are possible: a narrow read in which §2.3(b) is Games-only, and a broad read in which §2.3(b) is cited to gate MNA-pathway access. The bylaws themselves favour the narrow read; the broad read would need to be sourced from a separate VIOC interpretation or policy outside the published bylaws.

E. Practical implication

On the public documents as written, an under-18 athlete with no prior "represented country" record (under WS Reg. 19.14) can be a member of the recognised MNA, race in MNA-sanctioned events, race in WS events entered by the MNA, and accumulate residency toward VIOC's §9.1(b) Games-eligibility threshold concurrently. The Games-residency clock and the sport-pathway clock run in parallel; one is not a precondition for the other.

F. What remains uncertain

Whether VIOC has adopted any internal interpretation that extends §2.3(b) authority to non-Games competition; whether VISA has agreed to or contested such an interpretation; whether World Sailing has been asked to rule on the boundary. These are the open questions in §06 above. The honest answer is that the public bylaws of both organisations do not, by themselves, foreclose the sport-chain pathway — but a definitive read requires the written rulings, if any, that sit outside the bylaws.

The above is a structural read of public governance documents only. It is not legal advice. It does not name individuals. It is meant to surface the questions that, once answered, allow the right governance body to decide a specific case under the right rule.
